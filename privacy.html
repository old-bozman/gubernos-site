Gubernos

Privacy policy

Effective 3 November 2026 · applies to the Gubernos app for iPhone and Apple Watch and to Gubernos for Mac

In short: your tasks, notes, habits and health numbers are stored as plain files in your iCloud folder and on your Mac. The developer keeps no copy of them: no account, no analytics, no ads, no tracking. Two things go through the developer’s relay: a bug report or idea you send, which we keep and which can include a picture of your screen, and notifications from your Mac, which pass through without being kept.

Who we are

Gubernos is made by Alexander Safonov, an individual developer (“we”). Contact: me@alexandersafonov.com.

What we collect

We keep only what you send us in a bug report or idea, or through TestFlight. Notifications from your Mac pass through our relay without being kept. Apart from that, none of your data reaches us, and we can’t delete or hand over data we never receive.

Where your data lives

  • Your system folder. You pick a folder in iCloud Drive (or Obsidian’s iCloud folder). Gubernos reads and writes Markdown files there. Apple’s iCloud syncs them between your own devices under Apple’s privacy policy.
  • On the iPhone. The app keeps its settings, a copy of the folder for offline use and changes waiting to be sent, all in its own storage on the device.
  • Your Mac (optional). If you install Gubernos for Mac, the iPhone reaches your own Mac over HTTPS, at a public address your Mac gets from Tailscale Funnel. Tailscale’s relay passes the traffic on and can’t decrypt it; the encryption ends on your Mac. A pairing code keeps other devices out. The address, which contains your Mac’s name and your Tailscale network’s name, can be seen by anyone who looks it up. If you choose the private setup instead, your Mac stays inside your Tailscale network and the iPhone needs Tailscale too.

Notifications from your Mac

With Gubernos for Mac, alerts that start on your Mac, such as an agent finishing or getting stuck, reach your iPhone through Apple Push Notification service. Apple requires a key that only the developer holds, so your Mac sends each alert through relay.gubernos.app, which signs it and passes it to Apple. This happens by itself whenever an alert fires; you don’t send anything.

Each alert carries your device’s push token, the title and text of the notification, which can include a task’s title and the agent’s last message, and the task or habit it points to. It isn’t end-to-end encrypted: the relay, Cloudflare and Apple handle the text to deliver it. The relay doesn’t store it, and it uses your IP address only to limit how many alerts can be sent.

If you turn off the iPhone’s own reminders or check-ins in the app’s settings, those come this way too. To stop this, turn off Pushes through the relay in Settings → Notifications on your Mac; you then get no alerts from your Mac, and reminders and check-ins the iPhone schedules itself still ring. With an up-to-date iPhone app, turning off Gubernos’s notifications in the iPhone’s Settings also stops your Mac sending them to that phone, once the app has next reached your Mac.

Services you connect yourself

These are optional and run on your Mac, under your own accounts and their own privacy policies. Gubernos only passes your data to them when you use the feature.

  • An AI model (Claude Code by Anthropic, Codex by OpenAI or Gemini CLI by Google), on your own plan or key. When the app triages your inbox, answers in the chat, reads a voice note or runs an agent on a task, the relevant notes go to that provider. Read their terms, especially on free tiers, where a provider may use prompts to improve its models.
  • Google Calendar, Gmail, Contacts, Drive, if you connect them on your Mac with your own Google sign-in. The data goes directly between your Mac and Google. Agents may only read it. Nothing is sent and no event is created without your confirmation in the app.
  • Oura, if you add your own token on the Mac, for sleep habits.

Bug reports and ideas

When you send a bug report or an idea from the app, your Mac sends it through relay.gubernos.app, a small service we run on Cloudflare. The relay opens an issue in our private GitHub repository. You don’t need a GitHub account, and the report isn’t public.

  • Always sent: your text, whether it is a bug or an idea, the screen’s name, the device, screen size and theme, the app version, the time, recent errors (with task ids masked) and a random install id with no name or account attached.
  • Sent if Send screenshots is ticked, which it is by default: the screenshots you add, a copy of the screen you are on and the title of the open task. The copy holds whatever is on that screen, such as tasks, notes, health numbers and text you have typed. Untick it to send only the list above.
  • Reports made by voice open the same form, with your words filled in. Nothing is sent until you tap Send.

Who handles it. We read reports to fix the app, and we use an AI agent (Claude Code by Anthropic) to work on them, so a report also goes to Anthropic. Cloudflare and GitHub carry and store reports under their own privacy policies. The relay uses your IP address only to limit how many reports can be sent, and does not store it.

Answers. Our answers come back to the report’s task in your app. While a report is open, your Mac asks the relay for them, using a key only your install has.

Keeping and deleting. Reports stay in that repository until we delete them; there is no automatic deletion. To have yours deleted, write to me@alexandersafonov.com with the report number shown in its task.

If you use TestFlight, Apple shares crash reports and the feedback you send through TestFlight with us, under Apple’s privacy policy.

Children

Gubernos is not directed at children under 13.

Your choices

Apart from reports you send, everything Gubernos keeps is in your folder and on your devices: you can read, export, move or delete it at any time with Files, Finder or any text editor. Deleting the app removes its on-device storage. Disconnect a service in Settings, or revoke access in that service’s account settings.
