Gubernos

Privacy policy

Effective 8 November 2026 · applies to the Gubernos app for iPhone and Apple Watch, to Gubernos for Mac, Linux and Windows (WSL), and to Gubernos installed from a browser, such as on Android

Where this policy says Mac, the same goes for a Linux or Windows computer running Gubernos. Where it says iPhone, it also means an Android phone or another browser you pair, unless it says otherwise.

In short: your tasks, notes, habits and health numbers are stored as plain files on your Mac, or in your iCloud if you choose that, with a copy on your iPhone. The developer keeps no copy of them: no account, no analytics, no ads, no tracking. Three things go through services the developer runs: a bug report or idea you send, which we keep and which can include a picture of your screen; notifications from your Mac, which pass through without being kept; and, by default, your iPhone’s connection to your Mac, which runs through Cloudflare under our account and could technically be seen there.

Who we are

Gubernos is made by Alexander Safonov, an individual developer (“we”). Contact: me@alexandersafonov.com.

What we collect

We keep only what you send us in a bug report or idea, or through TestFlight. Notifications from your Mac to an iPhone pass through our relay without being kept. Your iPhone’s connection to your Mac passes through Cloudflare without us keeping it; we hold only the record of the tunnel itself. Apart from that, none of your data reaches us, and we can’t delete or hand over data we never receive.

Where your data lives

  • Your system folder. Gubernos reads and writes Markdown files in one folder. With Gubernos for Mac it is on your Mac by default (~/Gubernos), and Gubernos doesn’t send it to iCloud. You can choose a folder in iCloud Drive or Obsidian’s iCloud folder instead, and the iPhone app without a Mac always uses one. In iCloud, Apple syncs the files between your own devices under Apple’s privacy policy.
  • On the iPhone. The app keeps its settings, a copy of your files for offline use and changes waiting to be sent, all in its own storage on the device. When your folder isn’t in iCloud, an up-to-date app gets that copy from your Mac, without the archive folder, and keeps it out of the iPhone’s backups. The app’s settings and changes waiting to be sent can be part of those backups, like other app data.
  • Your Mac. Gubernos for Mac keeps a daily copy of your Markdown files in ~/.gubernos/backups: the last 14 days and 8 weeks. Your iPhone reaches it over HTTPS: see Your iPhone’s connection to your Mac.

Your iPhone’s connection to your Mac

If you connect an iPhone, an Android phone or another browser, the setup gives your Mac a public address like https://m-3f2a….gubernos.dev, through a Cloudflare Tunnel. Our relay creates the address in our Cloudflare account and hands your Mac the tunnel’s token once, without keeping it. Cloudflare’s cloudflared program then runs in the background on your Mac. A pairing code keeps other devices out.

What this means. HTTPS ends at Cloudflare, not on your Mac. Everything between your iPhone and your Mac, including your files and anything from Google, is readable at Cloudflare while it passes. Cloudflare could technically see it, and so could whoever controls the account that owns gubernos.dev, which is us. That account also controls the tunnel’s settings, so it could point the tunnel at something else your Mac can reach. We don’t store this traffic. Cloudflare may keep request data, such as IP addresses and page addresses, under its own policy.

What we hold. The tunnel’s record in our Cloudflare account: its random name and address, and the internet address your Mac connects from. The relay uses your IP address only to limit how many addresses can be made. To stop the tunnel and delete your address, run scripts/setup.sh stop-tunnel. If that can’t reach the relay, your Mac still stops the tunnel; write to me@alexandersafonov.com with the address and we’ll delete the record.

If you’d rather not. scripts/setup.sh phone --tailscale deletes the Cloudflare address and uses Tailscale Funnel instead: Tailscale’s relay passes the traffic on and can’t decrypt it, and the encryption ends on your Mac. It needs a Tailscale account, and its address, which contains your Mac’s name and your Tailscale network’s name, can be seen by anyone who looks it up. scripts/setup.sh phone --private keeps your Mac inside your Tailscale network, and the iPhone needs Tailscale too.

Notifications from your Mac

With Gubernos for Mac, alerts that start on your Mac, such as an agent finishing or getting stuck, reach your iPhone through Apple Push Notification service. Apple requires a key that only the developer holds, so your Mac sends each alert through relay.gubernos.app, which signs it and passes it to Apple. This happens by itself whenever an alert fires; you don’t send anything.

Each alert carries your device’s push token, the title and text of the notification, which can include a task’s title and the agent’s last message, and the task or habit it points to. It isn’t end-to-end encrypted: the relay, Cloudflare and Apple handle the text to deliver it. The relay doesn’t store it, and it uses your IP address only to limit how many alerts can be sent.

If you turn off the iPhone’s own reminders or check-ins in the app’s settings, those come this way too. To stop this, turn off Pushes through the relay on your Mac, in Settings → Your Mac; you then get no alerts from your Mac, and reminders and check-ins the iPhone schedules itself still ring. With an up-to-date iPhone app, turning off Gubernos’s notifications in the iPhone’s Settings also stops your Mac sending them to that phone, once the app has next reached your Mac.

On Android or in another browser, notifications don’t use our relay. Your Mac sends them as Web Push straight to the browser’s push service (Google’s, for Chrome), encrypted so that only your browser can read them. The push service sees when a notification is sent and to which browser, not what it says.

Services you connect yourself

These are optional and run on your Mac, under your own accounts and their own privacy policies. Gubernos only passes your data to them when you use the feature.

  • An AI model (Claude Code by Anthropic or Codex by OpenAI), on your own subscription. When the app triages your inbox, answers in the chat, reads a voice note or runs an agent on a task, the relevant notes go to that provider. Check that provider’s terms and privacy settings: depending on them, it may use your prompts to improve its models, whatever the plan.
  • Google Calendar, Gmail, Contacts, Drive, if you sign in on your Mac: see Google.
  • Oura, if you add your own token on the Mac, for sleep habits.

Google

Connecting Google is optional: on your Mac, Settings → Your Mac → Google → Sign in with Google. You can untick what you don’t want. Google hasn’t verified Gubernos yet, so it first warns that it “hasn’t verified this app” (Advanced → Go to Gubernos), and at most 100 people can sign in until it does.

To remove access: in Gubernos, Settings → Your Mac → Google → Disconnect. It revokes access at Google and deletes the sign-in from your Mac. If your Mac is offline, Google doesn’t get that message, so check myaccount.google.com/permissions. What is already saved in your folder stays until you delete it.

Bug reports and ideas

When you send a bug report or an idea from the app, your Mac sends it through relay.gubernos.app, a small service we run on Cloudflare. The relay opens an issue in our private GitHub repository. You don’t need a GitHub account, and the report isn’t public.

  • Always sent: your text, whether it is a bug or an idea, the screen’s name, the device, screen size and theme, the app version, the time, recent errors (with task ids masked) and a random install id with no name or account attached.
  • Sent if Send screenshots is ticked, which it is by default: the screenshots you add, a copy of the screen you are on and the title of the open task. The copy holds whatever is on that screen, such as tasks, notes, health numbers, mail and calendar events, and text you have typed. Untick it to send only the list above.
  • Reports made by voice open the same form, with your words filled in. Nothing is sent until you tap Send.

Who handles it. We read reports to fix the app, and we use an AI agent (Claude Code by Anthropic) to work on them, so a report also goes to Anthropic. Cloudflare and GitHub carry and store reports under their own privacy policies. The relay uses your IP address only to limit how many reports can be sent, and does not store it.

Answers. Our answers come back to the report’s task in your app. While a report is open, your Mac asks the relay for them, using a key only your install has.

Keeping and deleting. Reports stay in that repository until we delete them; there is no automatic deletion. To have yours deleted, write to me@alexandersafonov.com with the report number shown in its task.

If you use TestFlight, Apple shares crash reports and the feedback you send through TestFlight with us, under Apple’s privacy policy.

Children

Gubernos is not directed at children under 13.

Your choices

Apart from reports you send, everything Gubernos keeps is in your folder and on your devices: you can read, export, move or delete it at any time with Files, Finder or any text editor. Deleting the app removes its on-device storage. To remove your files from the Mac completely, also delete the daily copies in ~/.gubernos/backups. To disconnect a service, revoke access in that service’s account settings; for Google, see Google.
